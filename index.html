Months 1–2· In progress· Est. cost: $15,000–30,000 MXN
Why this matters: Everything downstream depends on getting this right. Click any task below to expand its workspace — check off sub-steps, add notes, and attach files. Your progress drives the Phase 1 bar on the dashboard.

Key decisions to make
General vs. specialty?
General labs have broader demand but thinner margins. Specialty (e.g., hormonal panels, molecular) commands higher tickets but needs more expensive equipment and a wider referral base. Recommended: start general, layer specialty at month 12–18.
Own vs. lease equipment?
Many reagent companies (Roche, Beckman, Mindray) offer comodato: free analyzer in exchange for reagent exclusivity. This slashes startup cost but locks you in. Good for year 1, renegotiate after.
Referral lab partnership?
For tests you won't run in-house (PCR, specialized cultures, genetic), partner with a referral lab like Carpermor or Quest Mexico. You draw the sample, they run it, you keep the margin on the patient-facing price.
Querétaro-specific tips
Best zones to research: Juriquilla/Zibatá (high income, low competition), Satélite/Epigmenio (dense residential, IMSS overflow), and Centro/Constituyentes (high foot traffic, physician offices).

Corporate channel: Querétaro's industrial parks (El Marqués, Aeropuerto) house 300+ manufacturing plants required by NOM to provide annual employee health screenings. This is a massive untapped B2B channel.

Key decisions to make
S.A. de C.V. vs S.A.S.?
S.A. de C.V. is standard for clinical labs — liability protection, investor-ready, expected by COFEPRIS. S.A.S. is simpler but has capital limits. Go S.A. de C.V.
Need a Pacto de Socios?
If taking on ANY partner: yes. Cover vesting, decision rights, profit distribution, exit mechanisms. Draft BEFORE the Acta Constitutiva. $8K-$20K MXN for a specialized attorney.
Which insurance?
RC Profesional (diagnostic errors), RC General (premises), Equipment (fire/theft/earthquake), Employee accident. Budget $40K-$80K/yr total. GNP, AXA, Zurich — bundle for 10-15% discount.

Key decisions to make
Full-time vs. part-time QFB?
COFEPRIS requires a named QFB. Full-time ($28-35K/mo) gives you on-site quality control. Some labs start with a part-time arrangement, but this limits your hours and inspection flexibility.
Hire a gestor for COFEPRIS?
A regulatory consultant ($15-30K) who knows the process can save months. They know what inspectors look for and can pre-audit your lab. Worth it for first-time lab operators.
Critical timeline
COFEPRIS is the longest and most variable step. Typical timeline:
Month 1: File Aviso de Funcionamiento
Month 1-2: Prepare Licencia Sanitaria application
Month 2: Submit application
Month 3-8: Wait for inspection scheduling
Month 8-9: Pass inspection (or correct and re-inspect)
Month 9-12: Receive Licencia Sanitaria

Key decisions to make
Buy vs. comodato?
Comodato: free analyzer, locked to one reagent vendor. Slashes startup cost by $300-800K but locks you in for 3-5 years. Good for year 1 — renegotiate after volume proves out.
Which LIS?
SaaS ($3-8K/mo) vs. on-premise license ($50-150K). SaaS is recommended — lower upfront, automatic updates, WhatsApp integration. Must support CFDI 4.0 and Spanish-language reports.
New vs. refurbished equipment?
Refurbished hematology/chemistry analyzers can save 40-60%. Ensure warranty, calibration certificates, and reagent availability. Mindray offers competitive new-equipment pricing for emerging markets.

Key decisions to make
Minimum viable team?
4 people: QFB (director), 1 quimico analista, 1 flebotomista, 1 recepcionista. Add a second lab tech at ~200 studies/month. Cleaning can be outsourced initially.
PACAL vs. RIQAS vs. Bio-Rad?

Key decisions to make
Launch with or without toma a domicilio?
Home collection adds cost ($10-15K/mo for a dedicated flebotomista + vehicle). Wait until you have 40+ domicilio requests/month to break even. Use the Toma a Domicilio calculator in the app.
Soft launch first?
Yes. Open 2 weeks before grand opening with limited hours and invite-only patients. Use this to shake out LIS bugs, QC issues, and workflow bottlenecks.
Opening discount?
10-20% off for the first month drives volume and word-of-mouth. Don\'t discount to physician-referred patients — the médico discount is already applied.
Querétaro-specific tips
Physician visits work best 8-10am and 1-3pm — between their patient blocks. Bring a printed price list (público + médico tiers), a sample lab report, and business cards.

Google Business Profile is the #1 way walk-in patients find you. Upload 10+ photos, set hours, add your test menu and prices. Respond to every review within 24 hours.

Mexico funding landscape
NAFIN — Nacional Financiera
Up to $2M MXN, 10–14% annual, 12–84 months. Via BBVA/Banamex/Banorte intermediaries. Requires solid business plan for new ventures.
SE — Fondo PyME / FAMPYME
Government grants and subsidies for SMEs in strategic sectors. Health services qualify. Non-reimbursable grants of $50K–$500K MXN. Competitive application.
Fondeadora / Doopla — Crowdfunding
Mexican regulated crowdfunding platforms (CNBV-regulated). Good for community-backed health projects. 10–18% effective rate for debt crowdfunding.
Angel / socio capitalista
Silent partner common in Mexican healthcare. Typically 20–40% equity stake for $500K–$1.5M MXN. Define roles, profit split, and exit in Acta Constitutiva.

Break-even chart
Revenue vs. total costs as volume increases — the intersection is your break-even point
How to read this chart
The green line shows monthly revenue as your study volume grows. The red line shows total costs — which is your fixed costs (rent, payroll, utilities, insurance) plus variable costs (reagents, which scale with volume). The point where these two lines cross is your break-even volume — the minimum number of studies per month where revenue covers all costs. Below that crossing point, you're operating at a loss; above it, every additional study contributes to profit.
The dashed blue line shows profit (or loss) directly — it's the gap between revenue and costs at each volume level. When the blue line crosses zero, that's the same break-even point. Notice the red line doesn't start at zero — that's your fixed costs, which you pay regardless of volume. The gap between the slopes of the green and red lines represents your contribution margin: the fraction of each additional study's revenue that goes toward covering fixed costs and eventually profit.

NOM-166-SSA1-1997 requires written SOPs for every test, calibration logs, internal QC records, external quality assessment (EQC/PACAL) participation, and staff competency documentation.
