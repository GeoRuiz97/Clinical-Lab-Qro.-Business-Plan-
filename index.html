Define business model
Direct patient, physician referral, corporate contracts, IMSS subrogación
Revenue mix to target: 60% direct patient walk-in, 30% physician referral, 10% corporate/IMSS. The physician referral channel has the highest LTV — a single referring médico can send 30–80 patients/month.

IMSS subrogación: High volume but low margin and slow payment (60–90 days). Only pursue once you have operational stability.

Deliverable: Written business model canvas with revenue assumptions.

Constitución de empresa (S.A. de C.V.)
Notario, acta constitutiva, razón social
~2–4 weeks · ~$15,000–25,000 MXN
Why S.A. de C.V.: Limits personal liability, has clean structure for partners or investors, and is the format COFEPRIS expects for clinical labs. Avoid Persona Física — too much personal exposure.

What the notario needs: Proposed razón social (check availability at SE portal first), partner names and CURP, capital split, and your objeto social — make it broad: "Prestación de servicios de laboratorio de análisis clínicos, toma de muestras, y servicios de salud en general."

Tip: The notario process takes 2–4 weeks. Start this the moment you have your location locked.
$15,000–25,000 MXN 2–4 weeks

RFC ante el SAT
Tax registration, CFDI setup, régimen fiscal
Process: Register online at sat.gob.mx with your Acta Constitutiva and e.firma. Choose Régimen General de Ley Personas Morales. This enables you to issue CFDI invoices (required for corporate clients and IMSS).

Also get: e.firma (electronic signature) — needed for virtually every government portal. Schedule appointment at your nearest SAT office.

PAC provider: You'll need a PAC (Proveedor Autorizado de Certificación) to issue CFDIs. Facturama, Finkok, and SW SAPiens are popular affordable options (~$500–2,000 MXN/year).
Free (SAT) + PAC fee 1–2 weeks
›
IMPI trademark (recommended)
Register lab name and logo
Why bother: If you plan to grow to multiple locations or franchise the model, your brand is a core asset. IMPI registration is cheap insurance against a competitor copying your name.

Process: File at impi.gob.mx. Choose class 44 (servicios médicos). Search for conflicts first. Filing fee ~$2,500 MXN. Approval takes 3–6 months but the filing date is what protects you.

Not urgent but do it early so the clock starts.
~$2,500 MXN 3–6 months for approval Optional but smart

Lease contract for lab space
Must specify uso de suelo: servicios médicos
Critical clause: The lease must explicitly state the permitted use as "servicios médicos / laboratorio clínico." COFEPRIS will check this during inspection. A generic commercial lease will not be accepted.

Negotiate: Rent-free period during buildout (2–3 months is standard for commercial medical space). Permission for structural modifications (plumbing for sinks, waste room, ventilation). Long initial term (3–5 years) gives you stability and leverage.

Deposit: Typically 2 months. Factor into startup capital.

Licencia Sanitaria — COFEPRIS
Main permit to operate. Requires Director Técnico, physical inspection, equipment list
60–120 business days officially — plan for more
This is your longest dependency. File as early as possible — everything else waits on this.

What COFEPRIS will inspect: Physical layout matching submitted floor plan, all listed equipment present and calibrated, QFB Director Técnico named and cédula on file, RPBI waste plan in place, hygiene and safety standards met.

Common rejection reasons: Floor plan mismatch, missing equipment calibration certs, incomplete RPBI contract, QFB cédula not registered with DGP.

Pre-inspection tip: Hire a COFEPRIS consultant (~$5,000–10,000 MXN) for a mock inspection before the real one. Saves months.
$5,000–15,000 MXN in fees Start immediately after RFC See: Regulatory page
›
Cumplimiento NOM-007-SSA3-2011
General lab organization and infrastructure standards
What it covers: Physical space requirements (areas de recepción, toma de muestra, análisis, lavado, almacén), minimum equipment, lighting, ventilation, and biosafety measures.

Key requirement: Each functional area must be physically separated — you can't take samples in the same space where you run analyzers.

Architect tip: Hire a local architect familiar with this NOM before finalizing your buildout plan. Retrofitting is expensive.
Ongoing compliance Plan buildout around this NOM
›
Cumplimiento NOM-087-SEMARNAT (RPBI)
Biological hazardous waste management plan
What you need: Written RPBI management plan, signed contract with an authorized waste transporter, red containers for sharps and biohazard waste, monthly generation log.

Transporters in Querétaro: ECOQUIM and STERICYCLE are authorized. Get quotes from both — pricing varies significantly by pickup frequency.

This document is required before COFEPRIS will schedule your inspection.
~$800–1,500 MXN/mo transporter fee Required for COFEPRIS inspection
›
Aviso de funcionamiento
Filed via COFEPRIS portal (SIVISAM) — required before opening
What it is: An electronic notice filed at sivisam.cofepris.gob.mx that formally registers your lab as an operational health establishment. Different from the Licencia Sanitaria — this is a notice, not a permit.

When to file: File this before or simultaneously with the Licencia Sanitaria application. The SIVISAM account and Aviso number are prerequisites for the Licencia process.

Cost: Minimal — mainly government portal fees.
1–2 weeks Minimal cost Portal: sivisam.cofepris.gob.mx

Equipment procurement
Hematology, chemistry, coagulation analyzers; centrifuges, cold chain
Order early: Import lead times for analyzers can be 8–16 weeks. Order during the COFEPRIS waiting period so equipment is ready when your license arrives.

Comodato vs purchase: At <300 tests/month, comodato (free analyzer, pay reagent markup) often wins financially. Above 400 tests/month, purchase saves money long-term. Use the Equipment Compare page to model this.

Minimum setup: Hematology analyzer (BHC), chemistry analyzer (QS panels), centrifuge x2, refrigerator 2–8°C, freezer -20°C, microscope.
$580,000–1,200,000 MXN (purchase) Order in Month 4–5 See: Equipment Compare

Hire Director Técnico (QFB)
Required by law — cannot operate without one on file with COFEPRIS
This is your most critical hire. Without a named QFB with a valid cédula profesional registered with the Dirección General de Profesiones, COFEPRIS will not issue your Licencia Sanitaria.

Options: Full-time (most compliant, $18,000–35,000 MXN/mo), part-time shared arrangement (some labs do this legally — verify with COFEPRIS Querétaro), or a consulting QFB firm.

Start recruiting now. Good QFBs with lab director experience in Querétaro are rare. Post at UAQ, UNAM pharmacy faculty alumni networks, and LinkedIn.
Non-negotiable requirement $18,000–35,000 MXN/mo Start recruiting now

LIS / software setup
Laboratory Information System, billing, patient portal
What a LIS does: Manages sample tracking, result entry, report generation (PDF with your logo), patient records, and integrates with billing/CFDI issuance.

Options for Mexico: LabSoft (popular in QRO), HealthPath, MedLab, or SistemaLAB. Expect $3,000–8,000 MXN/month SaaS or a one-time license ~$50,000–120,000 MXN.

Must-have features: WhatsApp result delivery, barcode sample labeling, CFDI-integrated billing, multi-user with role permissions.
$3,000–8,000 MXN/mo SaaS WhatsApp delivery is a differentiator

Soft launch + physician outreach
Visit 30–50 local médicos, offer resultado en 4h, free pickup
Physician outreach strategy: Visit offices in person. Bring a small kit: price list, business cards, a sample result report showing your turnaround time. Offer to pick up samples from their office for the first month free.

Your pitch: "Resultados en 4 horas, recolección gratis, entrega por WhatsApp." That's it. That's what they want to hear — their patients stop calling them asking why results aren't ready.

Track every contact in the Physician CRM. Follow up monthly. It takes 3–5 touches before a médico sends their first referral.

Key decisions to make
General vs. specialty?
General labs have broader demand but thinner margins. Specialty (e.g., hormonal panels, molecular) commands higher tickets but needs more expensive equipment and a wider referral base. Recommended: start general, layer specialty at month 12–18.
Own vs. lease equipment?
Many reagent companies (Roche, Beckman, Mindray) offer comodato: free analyzer in exchange for reagent exclusivity. This slashes startup cost but locks you in. Good for year 1, renegotiate after.
Referral lab partnership?
For tests you won't run in-house (PCR, specialized cultures, genetic), partner with a referral lab like Carpermor or Quest Mexico. You draw the sample, they run it, you keep the margin on the patient-facing price.
Querétaro-specific tips
Best zones to research: Juriquilla/Zibatá (high income, low competition), Satélite/Epigmenio (dense residential, IMSS overflow), and Centro/Constituyentes (high foot traffic, physician offices).

Corporate channel: Querétaro's industrial parks (El Marqués, Aeropuerto) house 300+ manufacturing plants required by NOM to provide annual employee health screenings. This is a massive untapped B2B channel.

Months 3–9· Critical path — allow 6+ months· Est. cost: $20,000–80,000 MXN

Equipment decision: buy vs. comodato
Purchase outright: Full control over reagent sourcing, negotiate better prices over time, no volume commitments. But: $500K–800K upfront for analyzers alone. Best if you have the capital or can finance through Nafin/bank.
Comodato (reagent-rental): Analyzer provided free by the reagent company (Roche, Beckman, Mindray) in exchange for exclusive reagent purchases with minimum monthly volumes. Slashes startup cost by $400K–600K. Drawback: you're locked in for 3–5 years and pay above-market reagent prices. Best for capital-constrained startups. Renegotiate aggressively at renewal.

Mexico funding landscape
NAFIN — Nacional Financiera
Up to $2M MXN, 10–14% annual, 12–84 months. Via BBVA/Banamex/Banorte intermediaries. Requires solid business plan for new ventures.
SE — Fondo PyME / FAMPYME
Government grants and subsidies for SMEs in strategic sectors. Health services qualify. Non-reimbursable grants of $50K–$500K MXN. Competitive application.

Mexican regulated crowdfunding platforms (CNBV-regulated). Good for community-backed health projects. 10–18% effective rate for debt crowdfunding.
Angel / socio capitalista
Silent partner common in Mexican healthcare. Typically 20–40% equity stake for $500K–$1.5M MXN. Define roles, profit split, and exit in Acta Constitutiva.
